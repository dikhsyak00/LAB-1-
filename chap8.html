     Chapter 8: Information Security and Cyber Law

Definition of Information Security
Information security refers to the strategies, practices, and technologies used to protect digital data and information systems from unauthorized access, modification, disruption, or destruction.

Core Objectives (CIA Triad)

Confidentiality: Ensuring sensitive data is accessible only to authorized individuals.

Integrity: Maintaining the accuracy, completeness, and consistency of data by preventing unauthorized alterations.

Availability: Guaranteeing that authorized users have reliable and timely access to information systems when needed.

Common Cyber Threats

Malware: Harmful software including computer viruses, worms, Trojan horses, and ransomware designed to damage or exploit systems.

Phishing: Fraudulent attempts to trick individuals into revealing sensitive personal data like passwords or credit card numbers.

Unauthorized Access: Breaking into computer networks or accounts without permission.

Cyber Ethics and Cyber Law

Cyber Ethics: The moral rules and principles governing responsible, fair, and respectful behavior online.

Cyber Law: Legal regulations created to address digital crimes, protect intellectual property, regulate electronic commerce, and enforce penalties for unauthorized computer activities.
